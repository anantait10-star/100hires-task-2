# Original Post
### Replace Judgment With Understanding In Sales Conversations

Judgment sounds like this:

> “I noticed your garage window is cracked. Why haven’t you fixed it?”

---

**“Why” puts people on the witness stand.**

It asks them to:

* Justify themselves
* Explain
* Defend

So they do.

They give surface answers.

They tighten up.

They protect their story.

---

Compare that to this:

> “Saw your garage window is cracked.
>
> Not sure if it’s on the to-do list or if it’s currently competing with the other 47 things homeowners are usually dealing with.”

Same observation.

No interrogation.

---

It replaces:

**Why → Understanding**

**Assumption → Empathy**

---

When people don’t feel cross-examined, they open up.

When they don’t have to defend, they tell the truth.

If you want to unlock more truth, stop making people defend themselves first.

# Research Notes

## Main idea

The way questions are framed changes how prospects respond.

Questions that make buyers feel judged create defensiveness, while questions that show understanding create more honest conversations.

## Pipeline Stage

Conversation → Question Framing → Buyer Discovery

## Key Framework

1. Avoid Judgment-Based Questions
- Avoid making prospects justify their decisions
- Reduce language that creates defensiveness
- Do not make the buyer feel blamed

2. Replace Interrogation With Understanding
- Acknowledge possible context
- Show empathy before asking questions
- Create psychological safety

3. Unlock Honest Conversations
- Lower resistance
- Make buyers comfortable sharing real situations
- Discover problems without forcing admission

## Potential Playbook Use

Use when designing discovery questions, cold call conversations, and follow-up messages.

Better questions help prospects reveal challenges naturally instead of feeling pressured to defend their current choices.
