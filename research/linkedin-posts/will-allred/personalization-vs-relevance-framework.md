# Original Post

Cold email personalization doesn't always "make sense."

Before LinkedIn gets out the pitchforks...

It depends on:

Target audience
Price point
Average deal cycle length

Here's an example.

Let's say I sell EHR software to optometrists.

EHR is like a CRM.

You don't just "swap" it out.

There's not a bunch of triggers that say:

"Now is the time."

Now...

If I'm selling to a big retailer like:

Walmart
Target
CVS

Might make sense to make that email 1:1.

(Might... lol it does.)

The deal size says:

Figure out how to make that email clearly written for them.

It might take a while to get them to change...

But the deal size (and stickiness) is worth figuring it out.

But let's say you're the SMB team at this hypothetical EHR company.

You're reaching out to small, independent practices.

You might know:

Where the ODs got their degrees
Their location
Their website
How many folks practice there

Theoretically, you could name drop ODs and personalize how you describe your hypotheses of the pains in their workflows.

But is the juice worth the squeeze?

Well...

Having tested it, I'd tell you the answer seems to be:

No.

You're better off investing heavily in product marketing.

Knowing:

Who typically closes
Who is typically involved
What they typically care about
How they typically talk about it
What their usual doubts and motivations are

etc.

These are going to have the biggest impact.

Then it's about how you craft the message:

Breaking patterns
Making it easy to understand
Making it easy to respond to

(There's an app for that 😉)

# Research Notes

## Main idea

Cold email personalization should match the target market and sales motion.

High-value enterprise accounts justify deep 1:1 research, while scalable outbound requires stronger understanding of customer patterns, pains, and messaging.

## Pipeline Stage

ICP Research → Message Creation → Cold Email Execution

## Key Framework

1. Enterprise Personalization
- Higher deal sizes justify deeper research
- Messages should clearly feel written for one account

2. Scalable Personalization
- Focus on common buyer patterns
- Understand recurring problems, motivations, and objections
- Improve messaging instead of forcing artificial personalization

3. Message Quality
- Break common email patterns
- Make the message easy to understand
- Make replying simple

## Potential Playbook Use

Use to decide when outbound campaigns need deep account research versus scalable messaging frameworks.

Personalization should create relevance, not just prove research was done.
